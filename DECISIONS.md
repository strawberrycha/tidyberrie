# Tidyberrie decision log

## 2026-11-22

- **Decision** — Accept any supported Node.js LTS (22, 24, or 26). Develop on v24.20.0 and declare `engines.node` as `>=22` when creating `package.json`.
- **Reason** — Node.js v24.20.0 satisfies the supported-LTS requirement and is already installed.

- **Decision** — Store `Account.openingBalance` in integer centavos in the Phase 1 Dexie schema. Derive current balances from received income, paid expenses, and transfers between the user's accounts. Keep transfers out of income and expense totals. Record each Received or Paid transition once in `BudgetHistory` in the same transaction. Use only outstanding items in the LEFT cards, show monthly net separately, and divide LEFT THIS MONTH by the days remaining in the period or until the next expected sahod for the daily card. Cover each rule in Phase 5 unit tests.
- **Reason** — Deriving balances lets undo and deletion correct totals automatically. Using outstanding items for projections prevents received income and paid expenses already reflected in current balances from being counted twice.

### Phase 1 foundation

- **Toolchain** — Added `@tailwindcss/vite` for the official Tailwind CSS Vite integration, `@vitejs/plugin-react` for React transforms and Fast Refresh, `@types/react`, `@types/react-dom`, and `@types/node` for strict type checking, and `@eslint/js` for ESLint's recommended JavaScript rules. The manifest declares Node `>=22` as requested. Use an up-to-date patch of a supported LTS line because the pinned development dependencies have narrower engine ranges.
- **Contrast** — Darkened Strawberry Milk primary to `#B4375B` and muted text to `#74525F`. Completed Sakura Night's semantic palette with dark on-primary text. WCAG relative-luminance checks for used slots give minimum text, muted text, and primary button label ratios of 8.78:1, 6.11:1, and 5.77:1 in Strawberry Milk and 8.88:1, 7.80:1, and 8.23:1 in Sakura Night. Focus rings reach at least 4.79:1 and 5.48:1 against used surrounding backgrounds. Control outlines against the surface reach 4.61:1 and 4.89:1.
- **Backup** — Version 1 exports all tables, including snapshots. Snapshot contents exclude other snapshots to avoid recursive growth. Merge uses the imported copy when records match, and Replace requires typing `REPLACE`. Imports use a 10 MiB limit and run in one transaction. Storage persistence is checked on this device after import.
- **Scope** — Home, Calendar, Tasks, and Budget use empty states. Settings exposes Phase 1 controls. Dexie v1 contains the ten requested tables. Transfer storage and balance calculations are deferred to Phase 5.

### Phase 2 tasks

- **Dates and recurrence** — Use plain local calendar dates for recurrence keys and store timed fields with their local offset. Monthly rules skip months that lack the selected day, and February 29 repeats only in leap years. End dates are inclusive and the maximum count includes the first matching occurrence. Save the configured weekday as RRULE `WKST` when saving a series. Changing the setting alone preserves previously saved rules.
- **Occurrence editing** — Offer “This occurrence only” and “All occurrences.” Defer “This and following” because safely splitting a series requires reassigning its exceptions and preserving count limits. Occurrence edits can carry their own fields and subtasks. Completed, moved, and skipped dates keep their original occurrence keys. A skipped occurrence can be recovered through Undo. Trash shows whole deleted tasks and series.
- **Smart lists** — Today and Upcoming show open items. Upcoming covers tomorrow through seven days from today. Overdue includes open one-time tasks and browsable 30-day repeat windows. All and Pinned present one card per series, showing its next open occurrence when available.
- **Storage** — Dexie v2 adds an optional occurrence override and a `movedTo` index for destination-date queries. The v1 definition and existing rows remain intact. Former backups still validate because the new field is optional. Whole-task deletion, restore, and undo run in transactions. Undo rejects if the saved group has since changed in another action.
- **UI** — Use native modal dialogs and keyboard up/down buttons for subtasks. The four existing pink/peach accents are joined by lavender, mint, honey, and sky tokens. Accent strips are decorative. Status and priority use text plus icons. No packages were added.
- **Contrast** — Strawberry Milk and Sakura Night validation text on the dialog surface measured 5.44:1 and 7.48:1. Status and priority text on the soft background measured 9.94:1 and 9.22:1. Selected list chips and primary buttons use the existing on-primary pairs at 5.77:1 and 8.23:1.
- **Performance** — Headless Chrome against Vite dev at 1280×900 measured 797 ms from starting the seed through showing the first page of 1,000 mixed-rule tasks. The page rendered 20 cards at a time. Click-to-observed-update timings were 214 ms for Today, 231 ms for Upcoming, 218 ms for Overdue, 141 ms for All, and 117 ms for the next page. These single-run timings include DevTools polling and development overhead. Task relationships use indexed lookups, list calculations are memoized, and recurrence expansion uses bounded list windows.

### Phase 3 calendar, Home, and Day Themes

- **Calendar scope** — Tasks are the single item type. Month and week use native buttons in an ARIA grid, with roving focus and arrow keys. Month includes dates needed to fill the first and last weeks. Agenda shows 14 days at a time. Date detail uses the shared native dialog as a phone bottom sheet and laptop side panel. Day view uses hour starts for quick add. Period navigation uses visible controls and grid keys.
- **Date handling** — Use the device clock for today and the current time. Update on minute boundaries and on visibility changes. A browsed date stays selected as today moves forward. Returning to Today follows the clock. Calendar opens in Month on reload.
- **Data and cache** — Expand Phase 2 RRULEs by visible window and apply exceptions against their original occurrence dates. A bounded cache holds up to 12 windows per live task-group snapshot. A changed snapshot invalidates the entire prior cache. Task completion and quick add go through the existing task services.
- **Day Themes** — Built-in presets are fixed. Dexie v3 stores custom definitions and assignments in the existing table and indexes assignment keys. Legacy weekday and date records retain their values. Dates override weekday defaults. Removing a custom theme soft-deletes its assignments in the same transaction and offers Undo.
- **Dashboard** — Count done and open occurrences together for today’s progress. Next up prefers today’s future timed task, then an open all-day or untimed task. Overdue uses the same query as the first Overdue list window, covering one-time tasks and up to 30 days of repeat occurrences. Older repeat windows are browsable in Tasks. Avatar mood prioritizes overdue items, then today’s completion state.
- **Design and dependencies** — Reused task and Day Theme color tokens and registry icons. Calendar dots are decorative, with numeric summaries and accessible date labels. Active date, focus, and action text use the existing contrast-tested primary and surface pairs. Added no dependencies.
- **Performance** — With the Phase 2 development seed of 1,000 mixed-rule tasks in headless Chrome at 1280×900, navigating from Tasks to the first populated Month took 298 ms. Click-to-render observations for Week, Month, Week, and Month were 39, 36, 14, and 32 ms. The initial result includes route loading and the IndexedDB read. These are single-run development measurements with DevTools polling.

### Phase 3B sticker assets

- **Dependency** — Pin Sharp 0.35.5 as a development-only dependency to decode transparent PNGs and encode optimized WebP on supported Node LTS releases. The browser uses static images and adds no runtime dependency.
- **Input and output** — Accept individual square PNGs from 256–4096 pixels with visible and transparent pixels. A shared key list covers the original task icons, all 13 Day Theme presets, and six avatar poses. Generate hashed 128px and 256px WebPs in `public/stickers/` and a typed manifest in `src/assets/stickers/`. WebP is the image format for supported app browsers, with SVG available for absent or failed images. A missing input directory preserves existing processed files. The combined reference sheets stay untouched and ignored.
- **Cache budget** — Cap precached WebPs at 512 KiB (524,288 bytes), divided evenly among registered keys. Lower encoding quality within the allowed range before rejecting an oversized pair. The current workspace has no individual source PNGs, so shipped sticker weight is 0 bytes and the generated manifest is empty.
- **Presentation** — Large illustration slots use lazy images sized 48–72px inside their fixed layouts. Small controls and date-cell icons use Lucide. Text identifies task and theme choices, so adjacent artwork is decorative. Avatar imagery has a meaningful mood label. The resting evening state uses sleepy, partial progress uses cheering, completion uses proud, overdue uses thinking, the idle daytime state uses happy, and Budget uses wallet.
- **Picker** — Both forms share a searchable radio grid with visible labels, roving focus, and arrow, Home, and End navigation. Saved icon keys use the existing task and Day Theme validation and storage. Existing keys remain valid.
- **Verification** — A separate headless build used four synthetic inputs whose eight WebPs weighed 3,240 bytes. It confirmed precached artwork, Lucide fallback, and task and Day Theme picker persistence across an offline reload. Production files and user source artwork were kept separate from those fixtures. Load Settings as its own route chunk so sharing the picker does not grow the initial bundle unnecessarily.

### Phase 4 reminders

- **Dependencies** — Pin Capacitor core, Android, and CLI at 8.6.2 and Local Notifications at 8.3.1. The CLI is development-only. Use app ID `com.tidyberrie.app` and the bundled `dist` assets.
- **Schedule** — Reuse the calendar’s recurrence and occurrence-exception model. Timed reminders use device-local wall time and subtract elapsed minutes. Untimed tasks use 09:00. Subtask offsets require a due time. Identity combines task, original occurrence date, subject, and offset. An edited time invalidates matching snooze or dismiss state. Tomorrow means the same local clock time on the next day.
- **Refresh and fallback** — Sync on task and reminder-state changes, launch, foreground, and a 15-second clock while the app can run. Enqueue up to 64 upcoming alerts by default across 14 days. Settings can choose 32, 64, 128, or 256. The Due list covers the latest 14 days. A late web timer shows the most recent minute as a system notification when permission allows. Older alerts remain in Due reminders. Snooze and dismissal live in a device-only Dexie v4 table and stay out of portable backups.
- **Android permissions** — Use the plugin manifest’s `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` and user-granted `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`. The plugin manages its reboot receiver and restores saved alarms after first unlock. Denied exact access uses inexact alarms. Reconcile pending alerts by ID and content when the app resumes. Android app settings are opened through a scoped native intent. Android system backup is disabled and the web CSP stays intact. The native bridge is injected before the CSP meta tag by Capacitor and native assets are bundled locally. [Local Notifications](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/local-notifications) · [Android alarm behavior](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms)

### Phase 5A budget core

- **Money and periods** — Parse plain PHP decimal text with up to two fractional digits. Reject ambiguous comma grouping, exponents, fractions of a centavo, and values outside safe integer centavos. Aggregate with BigInt so totals remain exact even above the per-record limit. Treat the entered income or expense date as its period date after settlement. Opening balances may be negative. Transactions must be positive.
- **Derived funds** — Keep opening balances, settled income and expenses, and internal transfers as separate records. Settlement changes status once, with the old and new accounts included in the same history entry. Projected values use only outstanding transactions. Safe-to-spend uses today through month end, inclusive. The sahod option uses the days before the next expected payday, with a minimum of one day and a month-end fallback. Fractional daily centavos round down.
- **Recurring expenses** — Keep a planned series and store each paid occurrence as its own row keyed by series ID and local date. Payment substitutes for that planned occurrence. Deleting or undoing that payment restores the projection. Series edits and deletion preserve existing settlements. Month, day, and year use their own windows. Future lists preview repeating expenses for the next 90 days and include later dated individual records. All records shows the series itself. Unbounded series make lifetime planned and Left overall open-ended. Finite series support an end date within 50 years or a count up to 10,000.
- **Storage and recovery** — Dexie v5 adds transfers and an occurrence index. Transfer arrays default to empty in older backups. Budget settings, notes, and series identifiers are optional for existing records. Each financial change and Undo appends history atomically. Budget Trash is restorable for 30 days and purged when Budget data is opened. An account with active transactions must have its transactions moved or removed before it can enter Trash.
- **Presentation** — Choose a default view on first run and configure card labels, colors, order, and visibility independently for Budget and Home. Keep period totals separate from all-time quick cards. Lists show 25 records initially. Added no dependencies.
- **Performance** — On headless Chrome at 1280×900 with Vite dev, creating 2,000 sample transactions with history and rendering the first 25 records took 1,490 ms. Updating the visible list after a search took 68 ms. These single-run measurements include browser polling. The seed control is excluded from production.

### Phase 5B budget extras

- **Dependencies** — Pin Recharts 3.11.0 for lazy-loaded charts and react-is 19.3.1 as its React peer. Chart coordinates are numeric centavos and may round at very large totals. Tables show exact centavos formatted as PHP. The charts load when requested, use theme tokens, and skip animation.
- **Category limits** — Match categories case-insensitively, count both paid and planned occurrences for the current local month, and warn at 80% or above. “Over” means the sum exceeds the limit.
- **Savings** — Create a dedicated zero-opening-balance account per goal. Contributions are transfers, so saved funds stay in Money now and never become income or expense. Progress uses the account’s derived balance. Projection uses net transfers into that account over the last 30 days and needs a positive rate. Archiving a goal keeps its account and funds available.
- **Utang** — Principals represent existing debts and have no cash effect on creation. I owe them posts a paid expense, They owe me posts received income, and Between my accounts posts a transfer between the two linked accounts. Derive debt state from those rows and prevent overpayment atomically. Removing or undoing a payment reopens the balance. A debt with active payments stays visible so the ledger links remain valid.
- **Bills and reminders** — Use Phase 4’s queue, notification limits, and permission behavior. Remind at 09:00 local time or the configured offset. Settling a recurring bill suppresses only that occurrence. Settling a debt clears its reminder.
- **Reporting and storage** — Keep Phase 5A formulas intact. The what-if preview adds an in-memory planned item and runs the same engine. The summary shows the selected month and six months of settled activity. Balance trend assumes current opening balances apply across the entire history and uses entered transaction dates. CSV exports active stored transactions in centavos, identifies series, and neutralizes formula-leading cells. Dexie v6 adds category limits, goals, debts, and payment indexes. Their arrays default to empty in old JSON imports.
